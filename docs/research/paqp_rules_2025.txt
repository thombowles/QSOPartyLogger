              Pennsylvania QSO Party 2025 Rules
1. Purpose of the QSO Party
    1.a. Pennsylvania amateurs operating in the State of Pennsylvania, without regard to the
         amateur’s home address, contact as many other amateurs in Pennsylvania, the United
         States, Canada, and the world.
    1.b. Non-Pennsylvania Amateurs try to contact as many Pennsylvania Amateurs as possible.

2. Operating Periods
    2.a. For the year 2025: 1600Z (1200EDT) October 11 to 0400Z (Midnight EDT) October 12,
         and 1300Z (0900EDT) October 12 to 2200Z (1800EDT) October 12.

3. Bands
    3.a. QSOs are permitted on all ham band allocations authorized by station licensee,
         including 630m and 2200m and any VHF/UHF/Microwave frequencies, except as noted
         below in Section 3.b. Primary activity for the PA QSO Party has typically been on 160m,
         80/75m, 40m, 20m, 15m, 10m, 6m, and 2m.
    3.b. QSOs are not permitted on the WARC bands (12m, 17m, 30m, or 60m).
    3.c. QSOs are not permitted via repeaters, satellites, EchoLink, IRLP, or similar VoIP
         services.

4. Suggested Frequencies
    4.a. Phone: Portions of the Phone Bands allocated to General licensees on 160M, 80M, 40M,
         20M, 15M and 10M. Use generally recognized calling frequencies on VHF.
    4.b. CW: 40 KHz up from the bottom of the band and 1810 KHz.
    4.c.   DELETED
    4.d. 160m High Activity: 0300Z Sunday (Saturday night 2300EDT).
    4.e. Other High Activity Periods (Sat and Sun): 10M – 1800Z (1400EDT), 15M – 1900Z
         (1500EDT), 20M – 2000Z (1600EDT), Phone on the hour, CW on the half hour.
    4.f.   Mobile Window: Keep these areas clear for mobiles:
           4.f.1.   CW: 5 – 15 KHz below listed suggested frequencies.
           4.f.2.   Phone: 5 – 15 KHz above bottom edge of General Phone band.

5. Exchange / QSO
    5.a. Sequential serial number plus PA county, ARRL section, Canadian section, or “DX”.
         Serial numbers may be sent out of order or skipped due to software serial number
         reservation systems. It is important to correctly record the serial number that was
         sent and correctly copy the serial number received from the other station.
    5.b. Logged exchanges must conform to the specified Pennsylvania County or the
         ARRL/Canadian section abbreviations listed below. The PA County abbreviations are
         listed at the following URL: https://paqso.org/pa-cntys-arrl-sects.html.
    5.c.   Multi-stations can send QSO number by band.
    5.d. Work stations once per band and mode.
    5.e. Work Rovers and Mobiles again when they change counties.
    5.f.   Log QSO time / date in GMT.

    5.g. Accurate logging is important. Accurate QSO time, date, sent serial number, and
         band/mode are as important as the received QSO exchange. QSO points may be lost
         for inaccuracies or errors in any portion of the QSO line.
    5.h. To receive credit for a QSO, the following information must be recorded without error
         and reported per the Cabrillo Specification (https://wwrof.org/cabrillo/cabrillo-qso-
         data/):
         1. Received and transmitted callsigns
         2. Received and transmitted serial numbers
         3. Received and transmitted locations as defined in Paragraph 5.a
         4. Frequency
         5. Mode
         6. Time and date

6. Scoring QSO Points
    6.a. CW: 2 points/QSO
    6.b. DELETED
    6.c.   Phone: 1 point/QSO
           6.c.1.   SSB, FM, AM, etc. Any voice mode allowed per FCC Regulations.
           6.c.2.   QSOs made using multiple voice modes on the same band only count once.

8. Power Categories
All power levels are defined as the output power of the transmitter and do not include losses of
filters, feedlines, switches, etc.
Power category is defined by the highest power used for any of the contacts during the Party.
Power levels are defined as follows:
     8.a. High Power: greater than 100 Watts up to legal limit
     8.b. Low Power: greater than 5 Watts up to 100 Watts
     8.c.   QRP: 5 Watts maximum

9. Station Definitions & Entry Categories
     9.a. A Home Station is a station that is operated from a fixed position. It does not need to
          be a domicile. Club Stations are considered home stations. Cabrillo files with
          Category-Station set to FIXED will be assigned to the Entry Categories 7.a through 7.l
          above.
     9.b. A Portable Station is a temporary station that is set up in a single county for the QSO
          party with the intention of tear-down post party. The use of permanent amateur
          station antenna(s) or tower(s) is not permitted by a Portable station. The typical
          portable station would be operation from a camp cabin, recreational vehicle, or tent
          with temporary antennas installed for the QSO Party. The power source is not
          restricted. Cabrillo files with Category-Station set to PORTABLE will be assigned to
          the Entry Category of Portable.
     9.c.   Mobile Station is a station that is capable of operation in motion with antennas generally
            accepted as mobile antennas. All equipment used for making contacts must be carried
            and/or towed by the vehicle, including antennas and power sources. Mobile stations are
            not required to operate while in motion but must operate within the Commonwealth of
            Pennsylvania. This does not exclude mobile operation outside of Pennsylvania, but no
            mobile bonus is allowed for out-of-state mobiles. A Mobile Single-Op station is one
            single operator, while a Mobile Multi-Op Station is an operator plus a driver, who is
            either only driving or another operator. A Mobile Station does not operate from a
            county line. Mobile stations submit a single Cabrillo 3.0 log containing contacts from all
            counties operated and their scores will be listed in the Mobile category results. In
            addition, a separate score will be shown for each county operated by the Mobile
            achieving more than 10 QSOs in that county; this score will be calculated from the
            QSOs, QSO points, multipliers, and bonus station points earned in each county on a
            standalone basis.
     9.d. A Rover Station is a station that is capable of moving from one place to another, but is
          typically using an antenna that is set up on site, then torn down and moved to another
          site. All equipment used for making contacts must be carried and/or towed by the
          vehicle, including antennas and power sources. Rovers do not operate as a mobile in

           route between locations. A Rover Station does not operate from a county line. Rover
           stations must travel 5 miles or more between stops to be eligible for the 500 point
           bonus. Rover stations submit a single Cabrillo 3.0 log containing contacts from all
           counties operated and their scores will be listed in the Rover category results. In
           addition, a separate score will be shown for each county operated by the Rover
           achieving more than 10 QSOs in that county; this score will be calculated from the
           QSOs, QSO points, multipliers, and bonus station points earned in each county on a
           standalone basis.
    9.e. A County Line station position is situated very near the boundaries of at least two or
         more adjacent counties. Ideally an antenna should cross the boundary, but this is not
         always practical because many boundaries are in the middle of rivers or highways. The
         station must be set up as near as practical to the adjacent borders and is fixed for the
         entire QSO Party. County Line stations may be a Home Station. Party officials are
         available to determine admissibility of any county line station setup. A County Line
         station sends a single report with the multiple county abbreviations (CAR/LEH). The
         County Line and receiving station logs a QSO for each county. Both stations receive
         QSO points for each QSO entry. If possible, the County line station should log these
         QSO’s with the serial number as sent. There may be small differences in the sent and
         received serial number due to differences in logging programs or the automatic
         expansion of county line compound exchanges into separate QSO’s. This is
         acknowledged and accepted. Mobile, Rover, and Bonus stations are not eligible to be a
         County Line station. County Line station scores will not be included in the individual
         county score listings of the results write-up. Note: The Cabrillo Standard does not
         include a station category for County Line but it can be selected during the log
         submission process.
    9.f.   Multi-Operator stations are stations operated by two or more operators. The operations
           may occur at a single location or multiple locations. All operators must be located in the
           same county (PA stations) or section (non-PA stations). Operators must share log data
           to minimize the potential for duplicate contacts, have only one transmitted signal on any
           given mode/band combination at a time, submit a single, combined log, use a single call
           and select one power category corresponding to the highest power used by any of the
           operators. Multi-stations can send QSO number by band. Multi-op stations at multiple
           locations may NOT send serial numbers by location; sending the same serial number at
           multiple locations by band may result in a check log. This definition only applies to the
           six Multi-Op and Portable entry divisions. All County Line, Mobile, and Rover Multi-
           Operator divisions must be collocated. The Cabrillo file Category-Operator field should
           be set to MULTI-OP for all multi-op categories shown in Section 7.
    9.g. A base callsign (normalized callsign) may be used in only one category for score credit.
         Any QSOs made using that callsign in additional categories will not count but must be
         submitted as a checklog.
    9.h. Single Operator High Power, Low Power, and QRP Mixed Mode logs must contain both
         Phone and CW QSOs that are determined to be valid after log checking. If the log only
         contains valid QSOs made using one mode, then it will automatically be reclassified to
         the appropriate single mode category.
    9.i.   Single operator categories are allowed only one transmitted signal at any time.

10. Multipliers and Scoring
    10.a. In-State Multipliers: ARRL Sections + Canadian Sections + PA Counties + 1 DX. Each
          multiplier counts once, not once per band.

    10.b. Out-of-State Multipliers: 67 PA Counties. Each multiplier counts once, not once per
          band.
    10.c. Final Score: Total points times Total Multipliers + Bonus Station Points + other Bonus
          Points
    10.d. QRP Operation Multiplier: Multiply QSO Points times 2. Applied only if the entry runs
          QRP power AND enters in a QRP-specific Entry Division or Entry Division that does not
          have a specified power. In other words, no QRP multiplier is applied to any High or Low
          Power divisions regardless of actual power used.
    10.e. PA Mobile and Rover Bonus: Add 500 Points to final score for each PA County you
          operated from where you made at least 10 valid QSOs.
    10.f. Accepted abbreviations for PA Counties and ARRL/Canadian Sections are listed on the
          PA QSO Party website at the following address: https://paqso.org/pa-cntys-arrl-
          sects.html.
    10.g. The PA QSO Party Association will use the Cabrillo file to adjudicate and rescore the log
          to determine the final results.
    10.h. JUDGING: QSOs will be checked for accurate sent and received QSO elements. QSOs
          that are determined to be bad after log checking will not be included in the final scoring
          determination. Report files showing the final score calculations will be available for all
          entrants after the results are published. A log may be disqualified for violation of
          amateur radio regulations, unsportsmanlike conduct, incorrect category selection, or an
          excessive number of bad contacts after log checking. The decisions of the Pa QSO
          Party Association are final.

11. Bonus Station
    11.a. The PA QSO Party will at its discretion have a special Bonus Station during the Party.
    11.b. The Bonus Station may consist of a single station or multiple stations in different
          counties as approved by the party administrator. Bonus Stations use a unique base
          callsign by county. Distributed Multi-Operator Bonus Station operation within the same
          county must follow the rules of Section 9.f pertaining to distributed multi-operator
          stations. Bonus Stations may not operate as a Mobile, Rover, or County Line station.
          The Bonus Station may consist of a single station or multiple stations in different
          counties as approved by the party administrator.
    11.c. Valid QSOs with the bonus station are worth 200 points plus a possible county
          multiplier.
    11.d. The Bonus Station(s) for the 2025 PAQSO Party is N3XF and crew, operating from
          Somerset County.
    11.e. DELETED
    11.f. DELETED
    11.g. Bonus Stations provide a service to the PA QSO Party and have an advantage
          competing on a level playing field. Therefore, Bonus Stations are not eligible for
          awards or records. They are also not eligible for first place in a county if there is other
          activity from that county.

          responsibility to submit a log that conforms to this format. A Summary Sheet is not

         required. The matrix which determines the Entry Division can be found here:
         https://paqso.org/pa-qso-party-rules.html. Check your Cabrillo file header and be
         sure to include a valid email address. Cabrillo log files are to be uploaded at the
         following web page: https://paqso.org/log-submission.html. Fill in all required

          info@paqso.org if you need assistance with log submission.

          of the PA QSO Party Association to review and evaluate that log, an entrant
          unconditionally and irrevocably agrees that he/she has:

         1) read and understood the rules of the contest and agrees to be bound by them,
         2) operated according to all rules and regulations that pertain to amateur radio for the
         station location, and
         3) accepted that the issuing of disqualifications and other decisions of the PA QSO
         Party Association are official and final.

         If an entrant is unwilling or unable to agree to all of the foregoing, the entrant should
         not submit the entry or submit the entry as a Checklog only.

15. Awards
    15.a. The award information for the PA QSO Party is located here: https://paqso.org/awards-
          certificates.html.
    15.b. A minimum of 25 valid QSO’s in the award category are required for certificates or
          plaques. This also applies to mobile or rover operators qualifying for an award.
    15.c. Plaques will only be awarded if there are 2 or more valid entries in the relevant category.
    15.d. A plaque will be offered to the entrant with the highest score and a Golden Log. The
          log must contain a minimum of 100 QSOs. In the event of a tied score, the plaque will
          go to the log with the highest number of QSOs. Certificates will be sent to all entrants
          with a Golden Log containing a minimum of 100 QSOs. The Golden Log award is not
          subject to the restriction of Section 15.c.
    15.e. Club Competition
         15.e.1.    Four plaques will be offered for sponsorship to recognize the highest club
                    aggregate scores, after log checking, based on the number of logs received as
                    follows:
                   15.e.1.i   In-state club – 26 or more logs

                   15.e.1.ii In-state club – 5 to 25 logs
                   15.e.1.iii Out-of-state club (US or DX) – minimum of 5 logs
                   15.e.1.iv Canadian club – minimum of 5 logs
         15.e.2.    Participants that want to contribute to a club’s aggregate score must correctly
                    identify the club’s name at the time of log submission.
         15.e.3.    Participants in single-operator categories may contribute their scores to a
                    single club and must be a member in good standing of the selected club.
         15.e.4.    Scores from multi-operator categories may be contributed to a single club. At
                    least one participant of the multi-operator effort must be a member in good
                    standing of the selected club.
         15.e.5.    The combined scores from mobile and rover operations may be contributed to
                    a single club; individual county scores are not eligible for inclusion in a club
                    aggregate score.
         15.e.6.    The published results will include a list of club aggregate scores. To be listed
                    in the results, a minimum of three logs must be received from a club.
         15.e.7.    Club aggregate score calculations include stations operating from any location.
         15.e.8.    A Club must have a Pennsylvania mailing address to be considered an in-state
                    club.
