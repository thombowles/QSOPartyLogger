FlexRadio DAX audio — sample rate and sample format, from a FlexRadio staff answer
=================================================================================

Source: FlexRadio Community (community.flexradio.com — FlexRadio Systems' own
support forum), thread "What are the audio stream specifications for DAX and how
do they compare to VAC in the 3000/5000?"
https://community.flexradio.com/discussion/6346756/what-are-the-audio-stream-specifications-for-dax-and-how-do-they-compare-to-vac-in-the-3000-5000
Replies by Steve-N5AC, shown on the page as "Community Manager admin" (Steve
Hicks, N5AC — FlexRadio's VP of Engineering at the time), September 2014
(reply 1 shown as edited February 2017). Fetched 2026-08-15.

Why this file exists: neither the SmartSDR TCP/IP API wiki nor FlexLib names the
DAX audio sample rate. This is the manufacturer saying it, on the manufacturer's
own site. Quoted verbatim; the parts about ADC bit depth, tuning-word accuracy
and the 3000/5000 are omitted as irrelevant to sending audio.

The question (author "st", September 2014):
> I understand that the native input and output sample rate for DAX connections
> is 24KSPS, correct? …

Steve-N5AC, reply 1:
> There are two types of DAX: DAX audio and DAX IQ. It sounds like you are
> talking about DAX audio so I'll focus on that. There are two ways to use DAX --
> you can connect directly to the API in which case you will be working with
> 24ksps VITA-49 data streams. You can also connect to the sound cards in the PC
> which have been upsampled to 48ksps. In the native API, the data is provided as
> stereo IEEE-754 single precision floating point data with 0dBFS at 1.0F give
> or take. So this is 64-bits-per-sample with a 23-bit mantissa.
> …
> The other way to access the data is after we have put it into a Microsoft
> Windows audio device. You will see these enumerated in the system as audio
> channels with DAX RX and TX names. This data has been upsampled to 48ksps, but
> is otherwise the same data.
>
> For DAX audio, the DAX data replaces the microphone audio so it goes through
> the compressor and the modulator. If you do not want to use the compressor, you
> can disable it. To remove the modulator and just perform upconversion, select
> DIGU which will essentially do what you want.

Steve-N5AC, reply 3:
> The equalizer is actually implemented in the CODEC for transmit and is only on
> mic audio today. So if you want equalization done, it needs to be done before
> the audio is provided to DAX.

Steve-N5AC, reply 4 (excerpt):
> If you need exact results, stick with the data out of the radio direct without
> resampling. We do this with DAX for convenience until a better standard than a
> Windows sound card is used for digital IF and audio data. We would like to see
> VITA-49 as the standard since it was created for this purpose.

What the app takes from it:
- Audio to the radio over the API is 24 000 samples per second per channel,
  stereo, IEEE-754 float32, full scale ±1.0 — the caller resamples the 48 kHz
  recording to 24 kHz and duplicates the mono sample into both channels, which
  is what FlexLib's 128-frame stereo packet expects
  (flexlib_3_2_37_dax_tx_excerpts.txt).
- DAX audio replaces the microphone and goes through the compressor and the
  modulator: the radio's own speech processor still applies, which is why the
  app does not process the recording beyond level and trim.
- The 48 ksps figure is the *Windows sound-card* face of DAX, not the API's.
